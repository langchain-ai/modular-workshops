# Urban Beekeeping

Urban beekeeping is the practice of keeping honey bee colonies in cities and towns, on rooftops,
balconies, community gardens, and in backyards (https://www.usda.gov/peoples-garden/pollinators/beekeeping).
Once unusual, it has grown rapidly as interest in local food, pollinator health, and
self-sufficiency has spread. Cities can be surprisingly good forage: parks, street trees, and
diverse ornamental gardens often provide a longer and more varied bloom season than monoculture
farmland.

Beekeepers cite several benefits. Bees pollinate community gardens and nearby green spaces, hives
produce local honey with distinctive neighborhood character, and tending colonies connects
residents to natural cycles they might otherwise never notice. Some studies suggest urban hives can
be as productive and healthy as rural ones, partly because cities lack the intensive agricultural
pesticide exposure found in farming regions (https://www.beeinformed.org/).

The practice is not without tension. Very high hive densities can strain limited forage and compete
with wild native pollinators, prompting some experts to argue that planting for pollinators matters
more than adding hives (https://xerces.org/pollinator-conservation). Many municipalities now
regulate colony numbers, hive placement, and registration to balance enthusiasm with the welfare
of both managed and wild bees (https://beeaware.org.au/urban-beekeeping/).
